# Policy mapping: a non-profit management liability package

This folder maps a policy fragment onto LATTICE's layers, mainly Instrument. It runs the reference instantiator over the mapping and validates the result against every layer's shapes. It then reports what the model holds and where it falls short.

**Anonymised.** The insurer is "Acme Insurance Company" and the insured is "the Named Entity". Names, addresses, policy numbers, the producer, product and section brand names, e-mail addresses and URLs are withheld. Amounts and dates are as in the fragment. The fragment's terrorism disclosure is summarised, not copied.

To regenerate, from the repository root, generate the stated meaning, bind it, re-serialise the binding, then validate:

```bash
python .local/policy-mapping/generator/gen_policy.py .local/policy-mapping/nonprofit-portfolio.ttl .local/policy-mapping/generator/part0-header.ttl .local/policy-mapping/generator/part1-meaning.ttl .local/policy-mapping/generator/part2-notice.ttl .local/policy-mapping/generator/part3-parties.ttl
```

```bash
python tools/instrument_instantiator.py .local/policy-mapping/nonprofit-portfolio.ttl https://example.org/lattice/instrument/nonprofit-portfolio/policy-v1 > .local/policy-mapping/bound-raw.ttl 2> .local/policy-mapping/instantiator-report.txt
```

```bash
python .local/policy-mapping/generator/serialise_bound.py .local/policy-mapping/bound-raw.ttl .local/policy-mapping/bound-meaning.ttl
```

```bash
python .local/policy-mapping/generator/validate.py
```

Validation takes about 20 minutes, since pyshacl's SPARQL constraints over 6,000 triples are slow.

The generator owns the parts that repeat: the wording tree and its variables, 19 exclusion clauses that expand to two exclusions each, 103 conditions, and the stored values. It checks that every scope, condition and filter it names is defined. The parts hold everything authored by hand.

## The fragment

| Part | Content | In the fragment |
|---|---|---|
| Front matter | policyholder notice, the claims-made notice | in full |
| Declarations | Items 1 to 8, with Item 6 giving each section's limits, retentions, dates and premium. The attestation | in full |
| Terrorism disclosure | statutory notice | in full |
| General Terms and Conditions | clauses 1 to 16, with 16(c) definitions of general applicability | in full |
| D&O, EPL, Fiduciary sections | | absent. Only their Item 6 rows |
| CPL section (in-house counsel professional liability) | 1 insuring agreements, 2 defence and settlement, 3 exclusions, 4 retention, 5 notice | cut off at clause 6, Other Insurance. Its definitions clause is absent |

Missing pieces matter. The CPL section's own definitions are absent: Claim, Loss, Defense Costs, Insured Person, Related Claim, Wrongful Act, Securities Claim, Non-Indemnifiable Loss and Subsidiary. So every condition reading them is a stated placeholder (C13), and the resolution filters are facts the claims file must supply.

## What maps, by layer

**Wording.** The fragment is one form of 121 elements: 102 leaves and 19 containers. It has a front, the Declarations, the GTC and four sections. Each declaration blank is an `wrd:EmbeddedVariable` (32 in all). They cover the party, the addresses, two policy dates, 19 amounts and five dates, each with a value space. The instance's assembled wording stores one `wrd:VariableValue` for each (law W5). Item 7's "Not Applicable" stays as text, because a variable with no value would breach W5 (G14).

**Quantification.** Money is in a US dollar space and dates are in a `xsd:dateTime` space. Three ranges are anchored at Instrument's context roles. Two are 30-day windows, one from the ending and one from a Transaction's arising. The third is a due date of 90 days after the ending. Item 6's dates are read by three interval conditions through placeholders: `ins:valueFrom` names the date word, and binding resolves it in the section.

**Party.** Seven role occupancies. The Insurer and the Named Entity are actors. The Insured Person, the indemnifying Organization and the spouse or representative are contingent: each is resolved from the Claim on each occasion, along an Eligibility path, through a filter (D17, I11). Subsidiary and debtor-in-possession are contingent with no resolution. "Organization" is a party word over three roles. "Named Entity" is a party word whose placeholder takes the Item 1 variable's value, the occupancy.

**Eligibility.** There are 103 conditions: 71 facts, 11 negations and 21 composites. There are also three interval conditions with placeholders. Every exclusion's scope states the clause's logic, with carve-backs negated (ADR-A103) and "or" as `AnySufficient`. Exclusion 3(a), for example, is "(not a Securities Claim, and any of four kinds of conduct, and finally adjudicated) or (a Securities Claim, and either of two kinds of conduct, and finally adjudicated)". Two evidence bindings read the claims file's dates.

**Behaviour.** One regime, the Policy Period, with seven states and eight triggers:

```
unsigned --(OnAcceptance by the Insurer: the attestation)--> issued
issued --(OnExpiry at the Inception Date)--> in force                    begins the instrument
in force --(OnExercise: Named Entity cancels)--> cancelled                ends the instrument
in force --(OnExercise: Insurer cancels for non-payment)--> cancellation notice
cancellation notice --(OnExpiry after 15 days)--> cancelled
cancellation notice --(OnExercise: Named Entity cancels)--> cancelled
in force --(OnCondition: Transaction)--> run-off
run-off --(OnExercise: Insurer waives by endorsement)--> in force
in force, cancellation notice, run-off --(OnExpiry at the Expiration Date)--> expired   ends the instrument
```

Cancellation powers apply only in force or in notice, so "may not be canceled after the Transaction" holds by construction. The return premium arises on entering `cancelled`. No transition leaves run-off for cancelled, so GTC 5's "no portion of the premium shall be refundable" restates what the regime already gives.

**Vocabulary.** The deployment adds three things. The first is 16 insurance activities, because the baseline has Pay, Indemnify, Report, Disclose, Repay, Terminate and DeclineRenewal but not Defend, Cooperate, Settle or Assign. The second is a term-class scheme with "condition precedent", bound to `ins-voc:TermClassificationContract`. The third is two policy-date roles.

**Instrument.** The stated meaning has 77 terms. Under them sit 35 definitions (27 from the Declarations, 8 in GTC 16(c)), 20 obligations, 7 powers, 5 prohibitions, 2 permissions, 47 exclusions, 4 qualifiers, 4 deemings, one sectioning and one regime.

- **Sectioning (GTC 1).** Four sections. The GTC are the general part, so they govern all four. Item 6 rows apply within their own section (`ins:appliesWithin`), so "Retention" and "Continuity Date" each have one value per section. The instantiator places 13 Declarations terms in D&O, EPL and Fiduciary and 38 terms in CPL.
- **Insuring agreements.** Two obligations, A and B, under one term and one trigger. The trigger is a covered claim: first made in the period, reported as required, and for Wrongful Acts. Their scopes split the Loss by whether the Organization indemnified.
- **Defence.** A duty to defend that ends on exhaustion or a refused settlement (`ins:endsOn`). An exclusion removes the duty for D&O-insured and Securities Claims. A separate indemnity covers consented Defence Costs.
- **Exclusions.** 19 clauses produce 38 exclusions, two each, because `ins:excepts` is functional. Clauses 2(a), 2(d)(2), 2(d)(4), 5(b) and 5(c) and GTC 5 add nine more. The holder is always the Insurer (law I8).
- **Deemings.** Two relation-back deemings, conclusive, for the four coverage relations. The mailbox rule, conclusive. The tolling-agreement presumption, rebuttable.
- **Classification.** GTC 11 and CPL 5(a) are classed as conditions precedent. The class is read-only (law I12): 5(a)'s effect is the "reported as required" member of the covered-claim trigger.
- **Qualifiers.** Retention, Policy Aggregate, Separate Limit and the hammer cap, all on the insuring term `term-ccp-1`. None carries its amount (G1).

**Bound meaning.** The instantiator generated 1,233 triples with no reports:

- 77 bound terms. 51 carry `ins:boundWithin`: 38 in CPL, 5 in D&O, 4 each in EPL and Fiduciary.
- Every party slot names an occupancy.
- Every Declarations placeholder is replaced by its value.
- Exclusions 3(c) and 3(e) are regenerated with CPL's dates, 1 December 2019, in place of their placeholders. So is 3(e)'s composite, along the path down to its interval.

## Findings

The gaps first (G), then what the reference instantiator does that looks wrong (I). Each names where it shows in the clause map.

### Gaps in the model

**G1. Amounts.** Qualifiers carry no amount, and no relation names a price. Twenty-six definitions give 15 value words their values, from the Premium to each section's Retention, and all are bound. Only the Retroactive, Continuity, Inception and Expiration Dates are read, by conditions. No amount is read. The pieces this blocks:

- Each insuring agreement's "excess of the Retention".
- The pro rata return premium.
- Reinstatement of limits on recovery, net of costs (GTC 8).
- The hammer cap: the refused settlement, plus earlier costs, plus 70% of later ones.
- Unconsented payments not eroding the Retention.
- No Retention for Non-Indemnifiable Loss or excess Securities Claims (CPL 4).

This is the deferred contract-amounts work. The policy shows it needs at least three things: a cap and a deductible bound to a word, arithmetic over occasions, and an exception to a qualifier, as CPL 4 has.

**G2. One limit over several relations.** A stated qualifier qualifies exactly one term or relation (§16.5). D10 lets one stated relation, split per section, share a bound cap. That is not what this policy has. The Policy Aggregate caps every section's insuring agreements together, and the D&O/EPL Shared Limit caps two sections' distinct agreements. Even within CPL, the Separate Limit should cover A, B and the Defence Costs of CPL 2(a), which sit under two terms. GTC 2's "highest applicable Retention" across sections sharing a limit is a rule over several qualifiers. This is the largest structural gap the policy exposes. A qualifier would need several targets when stated, or a way to qualify a set of terms or a part of the instrument.

**G3. Loss as a whole made of parts.** "Defense Costs are part of Loss." A and B divide one Loss "to the extent" the Organization indemnified. Carve-backs reinstate cover for the Defence Costs or the Non-Indemnifiable part of an excluded Loss. Scopes and exclusions are all-or-nothing on an occasion, so a partial indemnity that splits a Loss between A and B has no reading. The carve-backs are encoded as negated facts, "the Loss is Defense Costs", which is only right if Loss is itemised per component.

**G4. Related Claims as one occasion.** "Each Claim or group of Related Claims" bears one Retention, and GTC 3 brings Related Claims under one set of limits. Occasions are one per trigger firing. Nothing groups occasions into one.

**G5. Times with no range.** "As soon as practicable" (2(d)(1), 5(a)) and "immediately" (2(d)(1)) fix no range. §13.2 leaves a reasonable time to the case. So 5(a) states only its outer limit of 90 days. That limit is anchored at the ending, which is wrong for a Discovery Period, whose end is not the instrument's ending.

**G6. Discretion.** The Insurer offers a Transaction's Discovery Period "on terms … as the Insurer may reasonably decide", and the offer's terms are not stated. A Discovery Period itself, "up to six years", is a second period with its own effect: Claims first made in it are covered. The mapping has the election and its premium but not the period. It needs a state or regime after the ending, and the run-off has the same need (I-4).

**G7. Dates chosen when a power is exercised.** The Insurer's cancellation notice names its effective date, at least 15 days out, and the Named Entity's cancellation takes effect on the date its notice gives. `ins:after` fixes a period, and an exercise cannot supply one. "The earlier of the Expiration Date or the effective date of cancellation" (Policy Period) is the minimum of a word and the regime's ending. The interval condition `policy-period-span` therefore states only the Expiration Date.

**G8. Time at a place, and dates as roles.** "12:01 A.M. at the Named Entity Address" makes the instant depend on an address. The values carry a fixed −04:00 offset, which is correct for June but is no model of the rule. The regime's `ins:at` needs a `qnt:ContextValue` with a role, while Item 2's definitions give quantities. Nothing links the role to the variable: this is HQ-4, still open after C8. The date is stated twice, and the regime's copy is unbound.

**G9. Declaration values that depend on the case.** D&O and EPL give "Outside Entity Executive Coverage: Date on which the Executive first served". EPL's retention depends on whether the Claim is a class action, a third-party claim or other. A definition means one value per section. These need a value chosen by the case, or a word per case kind (the mapping uses the latter for EPL).

**G10. Consent.** The Insurer's prior written consent gates assignment, settlement, consented Defence Costs, a second panel counsel, and local counsel. That last consent is "not to be unreasonably withheld". Each is a fact condition here (`insurer-consented`). This is the C9b question: whether `ins:Assent` can name an act or exercise, not only a version. GTC 5's waiver "by written endorsement" is an amendment (C9a). The Named Entity's acceptance of the required amendments is an assent to the new version.

**G11. Meaning stated across several clauses.** A relation arises under one term, and a regime arises under one term. The Policy Period regime takes its states and triggers from Item 2, the attestation, GTC 5, GTC 7 and the 16(c) definition. The duty to defend takes its endings from 2(c). The owning term gets the meaning, and the other leaves look unassessed. Eleven leaves are of this "structural" kind. §18.5 has only "stated meaning" and "NoMeaning", so an unassessed leaf cannot be told apart from one whose meaning lives elsewhere. A status such as "stated by another term", naming that term, would close this.

**G12. NoMeaning for a clause that restates the default.** "Coverage applies anywhere in the world" binds, but its effect equals the model's default, since a relation with no territorial scope has none. NoMeaning ("binding nobody") is not accurate for it, and stated meaning would be empty. The same holds for GTC 12's first sentence. Both fit the status G11 asks for, or a "default restated" one.

**G13. Party details, and acting for others.** Item 4 gives two addresses, for general notices (GTC 9) and for Claims (CPL 5). `ins:noticeAddress` holds strings and cannot say which notice goes where. Item 1's address variable and the occupancy's notice address are the same value stated twice. GTC 9 makes the Named Entity act for all Insureds: it gives notices, receives return premiums, accepts endorsements, and elects the Discovery Period. `pty:Delegation` covers performance of an obligation, not authority to exercise powers or receive notices. The model has no agency. "Organization" has no acting rule, and the policy gives none, so a relation reaching it is Undetermined (CC-D10).

**G14. Master and underlyer policies.** Passport (GTC 6(b), Item 7) makes a section a master over local policies in other jurisdictions. That is a structure across instruments, and `ins:boundUnder` covers only an instrument created by another's power. Item 7's "Not Applicable" leaves the clause without effect for this policy, which a multi-valued variable with zero values would express. W5 asks for a value.

**G15. Rights the law gives, and non-parties.** GTC 11 restricts a right to sue that exists at law, not under the policy. `ins:impliedBy` can bind an implied term but not state a restriction on one. "No person … shall have any right to join the Insurer" binds strangers to the contract. It is encoded as the Insured Person's prohibition only.

**G16. Conformance to law.** GTC 13 amends notice periods to conform, applies the more favourable one, and withdraws cover to the extent prohibited by law. This is the precedence of law over terms (NRS N10).

**G17. Currency.** Payment in a foreign currency is at the Insurer's option, if the Named Entity agrees, or in dollars at a rate published on a given date. The rate is a context value from an external publication (ADR-A115). The option is a power, and the Named Entity's agreement is the assent G10 raises.

**G18. Definitions by reference, and additive definitions.** Continuity Date, Retroactive Date, Separate and Shared Limit are defined in 16(c) as "the date (or amount) set forth in Item 6". The meaning is the Item 6 row's, and the 16(c) leaf only points there. Transaction's limb (3) adds a section's meaning to the general one, a union within that section. `ins:prevailsOver` replaces, it does not add. The fallback order of 16(a) and 16(b) is section, then Declarations, then 16(c). That matches D12 and D13 where a section defines a word. Nothing checks that the order the policy states is the one the model applies.

**G19. Rights with no duty to except.** "The Insurer shall have the right to associate" (2(d)(3)), and the right half of "right and duty to defend". A permission excepts a prohibition, and there is none here. The mapping states the correlative duty, the Insured Person's duty to permit. That works, but it hides the Insurer's control of the defence, which is the commercial point.

**G20. Choice procedures.** Panel counsel has several rules: choose from a list the Insurer may amend, keep a delisted firm for the period, use the nearest listed jurisdiction, and add local counsel with consent. Only the obligation to select, the power to amend and the bar on unreasonable refusal are stated.

**G21. Knowledge.** 5(a)'s time runs from when "the Named Entity's Risk Manager or General Counsel first becomes aware". Exclusion 3(a)'s severability rule says whose knowledge is imputed to whom. An evidence path reads facts about the case. It cannot read who knew them, or attribute one person's knowledge to another.

**G22. Relatedness.** "Related Wrongful Act", "Related Claim" and "the same or related" appear throughout. They form a relation between case items that Eligibility can follow only if the claims file asserts it.

**G23. Other instruments.** Exclusion 3(d) reads what was reported under "any policy of which this … is a renewal or replacement". The declarations name the replaced policy's number. No link records that one instrument renews or replaces another: `prov:wasRevisionOf` and `fnd:supersededBy` are for versions of one identity.

**G24. Conditions precedent.** As C7c intends, the class is read-only and the effect is stated as relations. For 5(a) the effect is a member of the trigger's condition. For GTC 11 it is a prohibition. Both work. The effect of GTC 11's condition precedent, "full compliance with all of the terms", is a fact the mapping cannot derive.

**G25. A deeming's purpose stated as a question.** "Solely for the purpose of establishing whether [the Claim] was first made during the Policy Period" names a question, not a relation. `ins:forPurposeOf` lists relations, so the mapping names the four that read the question. GTC 3's "Related Claims … subject to the applicable Limits" reads it too, through a qualifier, which a deeming cannot name.

### What the reference instantiator does

**I-1. A definition meaning a condition with placeholders keeps them.** "Policy Period" means `policy-period-span`, whose bounds take their values from the Inception and Expiration Dates. The bound definition names the stated condition, placeholders included. In a scope slot the same condition would be regenerated with values, as 3(c)'s and 3(e)'s are. Law I13, that bound meaning names values and never variables, is broken for definitions whose meaning is a condition. Instrument's own I13 shape reports it: these are the only two violations in the bound meaning (see [validation.md](validation.md)). The cause is `_means_one` in `tools/instrument_instantiator.py`: it returns any meaning that is not a role or a placeholder as it is, without the copy that `_convert` applies.

**I-2. Generated conditions lose their evidence bindings.** `act-before-retro-date-binding` binds the stated condition. Binding generates `act-before-retro-date-policy-v1-cpl`, with the CPL date, and no binding names it. An evaluator reading the bound exclusion has a condition it cannot evaluate. Either binding copies the evidence bindings with the condition, or a binding names the stated condition and is read for every condition generated from it, which would need a link such as `ins:boundFrom` on conditions.

**I-3. A regime's trigger is never resolved, but the same trigger elsewhere is.** Regimes are stated only and never bound (C7a-Q1). `tmpl:on-transaction` names the word "Transaction". The regime reads it unresolved. GTC 4's offer power, which is bound, arises on the same trigger, so binding generated `on-transaction-policy-v1` with the word's meaning. One trigger now has two readings: the word in the regime, the condition elsewhere. Section-specific additions to Transaction (16(c)(3)) would make the two differ.

**I-4. Fixed schemes.** `ins-voc:ActivityContract` and `ins-voc:StateKindContract` bind Instrument's own schemes. A deployment cannot add Defend or Cooperate as governed activities. The 16 used here are ungoverned concepts, which no shape refuses. Nor can it add a run-off or extended-reporting state kind. Run-off is given `InForce`, so anything reading state kinds sees a run-off policy as fully in force.

**I-5. Formation when a policy is issued late.** Policies are often signed after their Inception Date. `issued → in force` is an `OnExpiry` at a date already past. Whether it fires on entering `issued` is unspecified (§12). If it does not, a late-signed policy never comes into force.

**I-6. Contingent parties outside an occasion.** GTC 11's and 2(d)(6)'s prohibitions bind "the Insured Person", which resolves only from a Claim. A prohibition that applies before any Claim has no occasion to resolve it. Insured Persons are a class defined by status, such as directors, officers and in-house counsel, and the class is not drawn from a case. A participation group, or a resolution from the instrument rather than an occasion, would fit better.

## Overall

Instrument carries most of this policy's legal structure:

- who owes what to whom, and when it arises
- what is excepted, with every carve-back
- when the policy begins and ends, and what a Transaction does
- which declaration value applies in which section
- how relation-back deems a Claim's date

Of 102 leaves, 77 have stated meaning, 5 are reviewed as NoMeaning, 11 are structural and 9 are gaps.

What it lacks is the policy's arithmetic and its grouping. The arithmetic is limits and retentions (G1, G2, G3, G4). The grouping is related Claims, a Loss and its parts, several relations under one limit, and several clauses stating one thing (G11). For an insurer these are the substance of the contract. The two instantiator findings, I-1 and I-2, are defects in the reference tool, not in the model, and each has a contained fix. G2 and G11 are the two that need design.
