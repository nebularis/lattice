Version 0.1, draft for review, 2026-10-16. A sketch of the claims module of LATTICE’s applied insurance domain (ontology/applied/insurance/claims/). It describes what happened, who says what about it, which contracts respond and how, what is owed, paid, reserved and recovered, and how all of that changes over time and is known over time. Nothing here is ratified.

1. Summary

A claim is where every other part of LATTICE meets. The peril vocabulary says what caused the loss. The exposure ontology says what was harmed and what it was worth. Wording and Instrument say what the contracts promised, Behaviour says what has happened under them, Eligibility decides what applies, Quantification and the evaluation context compute how much, Party says who stands where, and Persistence keeps it all, with its history and its privacy. The claims module adds very little of its own. What it adds is the structure that ties those together around a loss, and the vocabulary that makes the result read right to a claims professional, a lawyer and a regulator.

The design rests on ten ideas:

# Idea Where
1 Words with one sense. “Claim” means at least five things in practice. The model gives each its own term: the loss, the demand against an insured, the notification to an insurer, the claim matter an insurer handles, and the right to be paid §3
2 A claim is a case, never a class hierarchy. The insurer’s duties to indemnify, defend, advance and pay arise once per claim as occasions of the policy’s legal relations (CCS §7.6). The claims module supplies the case and its facts. It does not restate cover §7.4
3 One loss, many contracts, one shared fact base. A loss reaches a primary policy, excess layers, co-insurers, other insurance and reinsurance. Each responding instrument holds its own response, and all read the same facts §7.5
4 Facts, acts and judgements kept apart. What happened is a fact. What a party did with legal effect (a notification, a reservation of rights, an offer, a release) is a legal act. Whether cover applies, a duty is breached or a claim is fraudulent is evaluated or found, never typed in §5
5 The law’s own timing. Under English law the property insurer’s liability arises at the loss and the liability insurer’s when the insured’s liability is established and quantified. Claims-made covers trigger on the claim. The model follows the trigger the contract and its governing law use §2, §8
6 Money as movements, balances derived. Every reserve change, payment and recovery is an evidenced movement with a valid time and a record time. Incurred, outstanding, eroded limits and loss triangles are derived, on the evaluation context’s ledger §9
7 Settlement is a contract. A settlement, a release, a Part 36 offer and its acceptance are Instrument constructs, so a settlement’s terms are evaluated by the same machinery as the policy §7.10
8 Recovery is a claim run the other way. Subrogation, contribution and salvage reuse the demand, notification and settlement model, with the insurer as the party claiming §10
9 Workflow is the deployment’s. Handling stages refine an occasion’s arisen state (as occasion-refinement.ttl shows). Tasks, diaries and assignments stay with consumers (leftovers) §8.3
10 Privacy and privilege by construction. Claimants are often not the insured, often natural persons, and claims hold health and criminal data. Personal data, special category data and legally privileged material are classified on the record, not in the application §12

The model is drawn from the law first, then aligned to the open standards the market uses for claims data (ACORD, the London market’s claims arrangements and bordereaux, HL7 FHIR’s financial resources for health claims, ISO 20022 for payments, IFRS 17 and Solvency II for reporting), with a mapping through MORK wherever the standard is a format rather than a meaning (§15).

2. How the law answers the question first

AGENTS.md asks how the law and contract drafting answer a question before it is modelled. Each row below is general background, stated as a hypothesis for the design and to be confirmed by counsel before any rule is encoded (the whitepaper’s caveat in §3.8 applies to all of them). The design consequence is what the model must leave room for, and it does not depend on the detail of any one rule.

2.1 When is the insurer liable?

Cover Rule, as we understand it Design consequence
first-party indemnity (property), English law the insurer’s promise is to hold the insured harmless, so it is in breach, and liable in damages, as soon as the loss occurs (Ventouris v Mountain, The Italia Express (No 2) [1992] 2 Lloyd’s Rep 281, Sprung v Royal Insurance [1999] Lloyd’s Rep IR 111). The Insurance Act 2015 s.13A, inserted by the Enterprise Act 2016, adds an implied term to pay sums due within a reasonable time the indemnity occasion arises at the loss, not at notification. Notification and proof are conditions of the separate duty to pay, whose due range is “a reasonable time”, an open-textured standard read from a finding (CCS §6.4)
liability insurance, English law the insured’s right against the insurer arises only when its liability to the third party is established and quantified, by judgment, award or agreement (Post Office v Norwich Union [1967] 2 QB 363, Bradley v Eagle Star [1989] AC 957) the indemnity occasion arises on a determination or settlement record, not on the demand. The defence and advancement duties arise earlier, on the demand or on tender
liability insurance, US law the duty to defend is broader than the duty to indemnify and arises when a suit alleges facts potentially within cover (Gray v Zurich Insurance (1966) 65 Cal.2d 263). An insurer that unreasonably withholds benefits may be liable in tort for bad faith (Gruenberg v Aetna (1973) 9 Cal.3d 566) the defence duty’s arising condition reads the allegations (a some-value reading over the demand’s alleged causes, ADR-A103). Bad faith is a finding, and its consequences relations arising on it
third-party rights a third party may proceed directly against the insurer of an insolvent insured (Third Parties (Rights against Insurers) Act 2010) and, for motor, against the insurer directly (Road Traffic Act 1988 s.151, the EU Motor Insurance Directive 2009/103/EC art. 18). Many civil law jurisdictions grant a general direct action the claimant’s demand may name the insurer, and the payee may be the claimant. A statutory right is a relation ins:impliedBy the statute
reinsurance the reinsurer follows the reinsured’s settlements made honestly and in a proper and businesslike way (Insurance Co of Africa v SCOR [1985] 1 Lloyd’s Rep 312, Hill v M & G Re [1996] 1 WLR 1239). A claims co-operation clause that is a condition precedent stops the liability arising without approval (Scor v Eras (No 2) [1995] 2 All ER 278) already modelled by CCS C9b (reinsurance-claims-cooperation.ttl). The reinsured’s settlement is the arising act of the reinsurer’s duty

2.2 Which policy, and which period, responds?

Trigger Responds when Example covers
losses occurring the loss happens in the period property, most first-party
occurrence the injury or damage happens in the period, whenever the claim is made US commercial general liability
claims made the demand is first made against the insured in the period D&O, E&O, professional indemnity
claims made and notified the demand is made and notified to the insurer in the period, or an extended reporting period most London claims-made covers
losses discovered the loss is discovered in the period crime, fidelity
risks attaching (reinsurance) the underlying policy incepted in the period proportional treaties
long-tail theories (US) exposure, manifestation, injury in fact, or continuous trigger from exposure to manifestation (Keene v INA 667 F.2d 1034 (D.C. Cir. 1981), Montrose Chemical v Admiral (1995) 10 Cal.4th 645) asbestos, environmental
English mesothelioma the injury is “sustained” when the victim is exposed, for employers’ liability (Durham v BAI (Run Off), the “Trigger litigation”, [2012] UKSC 14) employers’ liability

Design consequence. The trigger is a contract term (a claims basis parameter, term-parameters §4), read against dated facts the claims module records: when the loss or injury happened, when the demand was first made, when it was notified, when the loss was discovered, when exposure began and when the harm manifested. A notice of circumstances under a claims-made cover is deemed, by the wording, to make any later claim arising from those circumstances a claim made when the notice was given. That is a relation-back deeming (CCS S25, ins:Deeming with ins:forPurposeOf).

2.3 How many claims, events or occurrences?

Wording Test, as we understand it Source
“occurrence”, “event” a happening at a particular time, place and in a particular way. Hours clauses fix the window for catastrophe perils Axa Reinsurance v Field [1996] 1 WLR 1026
“originating cause” wider than “event”: a cause to which losses can be traced Axa v Field
“series of related matters or transactions” a unifying factor connecting the matters, judged as a whole AIG Europe v Woodman [2017] UKSC 18
“all claims arising from one act or omission” the act or omission itself, not its consequences Lloyds TSB General Insurance v Lloyds Bank Group Insurance [2003] UKHL 48

Design consequence. Aggregation decides how many retentions apply and how many limits respond. It is a contract term evaluated over dated, located, caused losses (N7, substrate S4, contract amounts A28, A55). The claims module records the facts aggregation reads, and every grouping it derives is a derived artefact citing the aggregation term (§7.12).

2.4 Which cause governs?

Settled in the whitepaper §3.8 and the exposure ontology: proximate cause (Leyland Shipping v Norwich Union [1918] AC 350), concurrent causes with one excluded (Wayne Tank v Employers Liability [1974] QB 57), and the US efficient proximate cause rule, with anti-concurrent causation clauses enforced in some states. The claims module records the chain of causes and selects the rule by an evaluation profile chosen by governing law and wording (N10, AIR-6.4, W-Q2).

2.5 What may an insured lose, and how?

Matter Rule, as we understand it Design consequence
fraudulent claim the insurer need not pay the claim and may recover sums paid, and may treat the contract as terminated from the fraud (Insurance Act 2015 s.12). A lie that is irrelevant to the recoverability of the claim (a “collateral lie”) is not a fraudulent claim (Versloot Dredging v HDI Gerling [2016] UKSC 45) fraud is a finding. Its consequences are relations arising on the finding. Indicators are assessments, never findings (§11.2)
breach of a claims condition the consequence depends on how the term is classified: a condition precedent defeats the claim, an innominate term gives damages or, where serious, rejection (CCS S15, ins:classification) the breach chain is stated by relations arising on breach
late payment damages for breach of the implied term in s.13A, subject to the insurer’s reasonable grounds for disputing the claim a finding, with the s.13A term ins:impliedBy the statute
average (underinsurance) the insured is its own insurer for the uninsured proportion (Marine Insurance Act 1906 s.81, and pro rata average clauses) a combinator over the sum insured and the value at risk (proRata, evaluation context §4)
contribution an insured with double insurance is not paid twice, and insurers share rateably (Marine Insurance Act 1906 s.80) other insurance is a responding instrument (§7.5), and the share a combinator
subrogation the insurer who has indemnified stands in the insured’s place against third parties (Castellain v Preston (1883) 11 QBD 380, Marine Insurance Act 1906 s.79) a deeming that the payer stands in the creditor’s place (CCS S34), and a recovery demand (§10.1)

Regime Requirement, in outline Design consequence
UK FCA ICOBS 8.1 handle claims promptly and fairly, give reasonable guidance, not unreasonably reject a claim, settle promptly once terms are agreed duties ins:impliedBy the rule, with derived breaches feeding conduct reporting

Their occasions, due ranges and breaches are evaluated like any contractual duty, so conduct monitoring is a query over occasions, not a separate system.

3. The words, one sense each

CCS’s first test is that a contract lawyer or an engineer surveying the T-Box meets no word used in two senses. “Claim” fails that test in ordinary use:

the insurer’s file and handling of the matter “the claim is under review”, “claim number 12345” clm:Claim (§7.4)

“Instantiation” and “instantiator” keep the sense AGENTS.md gives them, and the claims module uses neither for claims processing.

7. The model

7.1 Loss

clm:Loss ⊑ fnd:Evidenced ⊓ fnd:TemporallyScoped
    ≥1 clm:lossDate…       when it happened: a time, or a span for a continuing loss
    ≤1 clm:lossAt          → aeo:Location        where, when it has a place
    ≥0 clm:hasCause        → aeo:LossCause       one node per link of the cause chain (AIR-4.4)
    ≥0 clm:affects         → aeo:Asset ⊔ aeo:Interest ⊔ pty:Actor    what or who was harmed
    ≤1 clm:inLossEvent     → icm:LossEvent       an event in the world it belongs to
    ≥0 clm:harmSubject     ⊑ icm:harmSubject     property, persons, data, financial interest

A loss is what happened, before anyone claims anything. Its temporal scope carries the dates a trigger may read, as distinct properties, because they differ and a contract picks one:

Property Meaning Read by
clm:occurredAt when the damage or injury happened, or began losses occurring, occurrence triggers
clm:manifestedAt when it became apparent manifestation trigger, latent defects
clm:discoveredAt when the insured discovered it losses discovered
clm:exposureFrom, clm:exposureTo the span of exposure to the cause exposure and continuous triggers, the Trigger litigation

Each date is evidenced and may be estimated, with its precision as metadata (as CCS §7.4.1 does for a proposal’s values). An unknown date is absent, not guessed (CL8). Neither ACORD’s claims messages nor the Lloyd’s claims bordereau carries the manifestation, discovery or exposure dates (references §8), so a claim whose trigger reads them takes them from its documents or from a person, and stays Undetermined until it does.

The cause chain reuses the exposure module’s aeo:LossCause (AIR-4.4), with the recorded and effective characteristics and the lifted peril already decided. A claim’s loss and a loss history’s loss record therefore read the same cause model, and the loop of §13.3 needs no translation.

7.2 Demand

A demand is an assertion, by a claimant, that someone owes them something. Liability, D&O, E&O and cyber covers respond to demands against the insured. A complaint to an insurer, and a recovery action by an insurer, are demands too (§10, §11).

clm:Demand ⊑ fnd:Evidenced ⊓ fnd:TemporallyScoped
    =1 clm:madeAt              when first made (the claims-made trigger reads it)
    ≥1 clm:claimantOcc         → pty:RoleOccupancy in icm-voc:Claimant
    ≥1 clm:againstOcc          → pty:RoleOccupancy in icm-voc:LiableParty   who is alleged liable
    ≥0 clm:harmedOcc           → pty:RoleOccupancy in icm-voc:HarmedParty
    ≥0 clm:alleges             → aeo:LossCause        the wrongful acts or causes alleged
    ≥0 clm:arisesFromLoss      → clm:Loss
    =1 clm:demandForm          written demand, suit, arbitration, regulatory investigation, subpoena, complaint
    ≥0 clm:reliefSought        → clm:Relief           damages (a quantum), declaration, injunction, specific performance
    ≤1 clm:inProceedings       → clm:Proceedings      when it is litigated or arbitrated (§11.1)

The roles are those of ADR-A102. A demand by an insured entity against its own director has the entity in the claimant role and the director in the liable role, and the insured-versus-insured reading of term-parameters §7.3 derives from that, never from a flag. Allegations are aeo:LossCause links too: a securities claim alleges misrepresentation (H.WRA.MIS), a breach of duty alleges H.WRA.MGT. The duty to defend reads them with a some-value reading (ADR-A103), because a suit with one potentially covered allegation is defended in full under US law (§2.1).

7.3 Notification

A notification is a legal act: an insured, or someone for it, tells an insurer of a loss, a demand or a circumstance. It is an ins:Declaration (Instrument §6.4), so its time, its maker, its evidence and its case are what Instrument already records.

clm:Notification ⊑ ins:Declaration
    =1 ins:actBy            the notifying occupancy (insured, broker, claimant under direct action)
    ≥1 clm:notifiedTo       → pty:RoleOccupancy     the insurer, its lead, or an agent named for notice
    =1 clm:notificationKind notice of loss, notice of demand, notice of circumstances, proof of loss
    ≥1 clm:notifies         → clm:Loss ⊔ clm:Demand ⊔ clm:Circumstance
    ≤1 ins:forCase          → clm:Claim             the claim it opens or adds to
    ≥0 clm:viaChannel       concept: written, electronic message, portal, telephone

The contract decides what a notification does. A notice duty arises on the demand and falls due within a period (CCS §5.5 shows the whole chain). A notice of circumstances is deemed to make later demands claims made at its time (a relation-back ins:Deeming, CCS S25). Whether the notice was valid, given to the right person in the right form in time, is evaluated from these facts and the wording (CCS S27, powers with procedural conditions).

A circumstance names the populations that might claim (exposure’s counterparty populations, AIR-4.5), never invented claimants (ADR-A102 decision 2).

7.4 Claim

The claim is the matter an insurer handles, and the case its policy’s relations are evaluated for (CCS §5.3, ins:forCase).

clm:Claim ⊑ fnd:Version ⊓ fnd:Governable ⊓ fnd:Evidenced
    =1 fnd:hasIdentity        → clm:ClaimIdentity      persists across versions and reopenings
    ≥1 clm:openedBy           → clm:Notification
    ≥0 clm:concernsLoss       → clm:Loss
    ≥0 clm:concernsDemand     → clm:Demand
    ≥0 clm:hasResponse        → clm:Response           one per responding instrument version (§7.5)
    ≥0 clm:hasLossItem        → clm:LossItem           heads of loss and their quanta (§7.6)
    ≥0 clm:relatedTo          → clm:Claim              asserted relatedness, for a person to judge
    ≥0 fnd:naturalKey         the insurer's claim number, a coverholder's or TPA's claim reference
                               (CRS CR0104), a London market claim reference, each a key of its own
                               scheme (ADR-A114)

A claim is versioned. Its facts change as they are learned (a second injured party, a revised date of loss, an amended allegation), and each change is a new version superseding the last, so the claim as known at a reserving date or a coverage decision is recoverable (CL5). Its governance state is the record’s (draft while being set up, active, closed, reopened as a new version with a new governance state). Whether the insurer’s duties are performed or breached is never this state: it is the occasions’ (§8).

One notification, one claim, by default. Related notifications may be joined to one claim, or a claim split, as the handler decides, recorded as a new version with evidence. Whether claims are one for a limit or retention is the contract’s aggregation, derived (§7.12), and independent of how the handler files them. Keeping the two apart lets a deployment file by its own practice without changing what the contract says.

7.5 Response: one claim, many instruments

A loss commonly reaches several instruments at once: a primary policy and excess layers, a co-insured placement with a lead and followers, two policies that both cover it (other insurance), a policy and its reinsurance, an employer’s policy and an employee’s personal cover. Each responds on its own terms. The model keeps one claim and one fact base, and gives each responding instrument version a response:

clm:Response ⊑ fnd:Evidenced
    =1 clm:responseOf        → clm:Claim
    =1 clm:underInstrument   → ins:Instrument         the version in force for the trigger date (§8.1)
    ≥0 clm:underSection      → wrd:Element            the sections the claim falls in (CCS §5.10)
    ≥0 clm:insurerOcc        → pty:RoleOccupancy ⊔ pty:ParticipationGroup
    ≥0 clm:position          → clm:CoveragePosition   what the insurer has said (§7.7)

The response holds nothing about cover itself. Cover is the occasions of the instrument’s relations for the claim, which the runtime evaluator creates and moves (CCS §7.6). A response is the index from a claim to an instrument, and the subject that per-instrument facts hang from: the position, the reserves, the payments and the handling stages of that insurer.

7.6 Loss items and quantum

A claim’s value is built from loss items: the heads of loss, each harmed thing, each claimant’s damages. Each carries its quanta as they evolve.

The quanta of one item coexist, each from its source. Which one a calculation uses is a resolution policy (substrate S7), recorded as a derived artefact, never by overwriting. Business interruption uses the exposure valuation’s rate-and-horizon form (asset exposure sketch §5.6), so an indemnity period is tested against the same shape the exposure set declared.

7.6.1 Bodily injury

Bodily injury heads follow the law of damages, which varies by jurisdiction. The reference scheme names the common English heads (general damages for pain, suffering and loss of amenity, special damages, past and future loss of earnings, care and assistance, accommodation, and periodical payments under the Damages Act 1996 s.2). A future loss is a quantum whose amount is computed: a multiplicand and a multiplier from the Ogden tables at the prescribed discount rate. That computation is an ins:computedBy target (CCS §7.9) on the evaluation context’s combinators, and a case with no computation available evaluates Undetermined (CL8). Injury coding (WHO ICD-11, and SNOMED CT where a deployment holds a licence) is an attribute of the injured person’s medical evidence, special category data (§12.2).

7.7 Coverage position

An insurer’s stated position is a legal act, and the evaluated coverage is a judgement. They often differ, for good reasons (the insurer has not yet seen the facts, or disputes the evaluator’s reading), and a claims model that merges them loses the record of what was said.

clm:CoveragePosition ⊑ ins:Declaration
    =1 ins:actBy           the insurer, or its agent with authority
    =1 ins:forCase         the claim
    =1 clm:positionKind    acceptance, acceptance in part, reservation of rights, declinature,
                           avoidance, rescission, non-waiver agreement
    ≥0 clm:citesTerm       → ins:Term        the clauses relied on
    ≥0 clm:reservedMatter  → the questions reserved

The evaluated coverage is the set of occasions and their decisions, each traced to its relations and terms (CCS §6.5), and so to the wording. A shape can report a declinature that cites no term, and a query can list positions the evaluation does not support, for a person to review. A waiver or an estoppel arising from a position is a finding (CCS §6.4), not a rule the model applies.

7.11 Parametric and index triggers

A parametric cover pays on a measured value crossing a threshold, with no loss to prove. An industry loss warranty pays on an industry loss index. Neither needs a demand or a loss quantum, and both are already within reach:

Construct LATTICE construct
a measured intensity at a site or in a box an intensity measure of the peril vocabulary (AIR-2.6) on its value space
the trigger and its payout schedule an Eligibility interval condition, and a payout combinator (contract amounts A57)
the reporting agency’s published value an evidenced finding, fnd:assertedBy the agency
an industry loss index a clm:Quantum of stage determined, asserted by the index provider (for example PCS or PERILS), for the loss event

The claim for a parametric cover is a claim with a loss, a loss event and a determination, and no demand. Its payment duty arises on the determination. CL-Q10.

9.4 Reserves

A reserve is an estimate, and the model keeps it as one: who set it, on what basis, when, and with what confidence or range where the handler gives one. Case reserves are in scope. Reserves for claims not yet reported (IBNR) and portfolio reserving are actuarial work over the book, out of scope here. What the claims module gives that work is clean, bitemporal movements from which triangles are derived (§13.1).

9.5 Currencies and taxes

A claim may be paid in the instrument currency or another at a rate on the date the obligation is established (contract amounts A48), and reported in a third. Every conversion is a qnt:Conversion under a context at a date (ADR-A95), recorded with the movement. Insurance premium tax does not apply to claims, but claims payments carry VAT treatment on repairs, withholding on some payments and sanctions screening on every payee (§11.3), each a classification or a derived artefact.

11.1 Proceedings, determinations and complaints

Every outcome of proceedings is a finding (CCS §6.4): a judgment, an award, an expert’s determination, an ombudsman’s decision. Findings are what the policy’s conditions read when the wording says “established by final, non-appealable adjudication” (PKG D&O 4.B(1), CCS §5.7). 11.3 Sanctions and payments

Every payee is screened before payment. A sanctions clause (for example the LMA’s sanction limitation and exclusion clause) is an exclusion whose scope reads that outcome. A payment made with no screening run is reported.

11.4 Conduct duties, monitored by query

Regulatory duties are relations ins:impliedBy the rule (§2.6), so “claims not acknowledged within the state’s period”, “settlements agreed but unpaid after a reasonable time”, “declinatures without cited terms” are queries over occasions and positions. A conduct report is a derived artefact over them, with its read set, and every row traces to a claim, an occasion and the rule.

12. Evidence, documents and personal data

12.1 Evidence and documents

Every fact cites fnd:Evidence: the proof of loss, an adjuster’s report, a medical report, an invoice, a photograph, a court order, a telephone note. Documents are stored outside the graph and cited by external key (ADR-A114), with their kind from a clm-voc: scheme and their hash.

12.2 Personal and special category data

A claimant is often a natural person who is not the insured, never consented to the policy, and whose data includes health (GDPR art. 9) and sometimes criminal offence data (art. 10). Children are claimants too. The record classifies this where it is stored:

Claims messages between insureds, brokers, insurers, TPAs and reinsurers are formats, so each is a MORK mapping graph per format version, compiled to RML, with the mapping as evidence on what it produced (mork-bridge §6). Codes in them (cause of loss, head of loss, status) go through the same crosswalks as contract and exposure codes, so a loss coded in a bordereau and a peril excluded in a wording are compared in one scheme (CL7). §15 lists the formats.

13.3 The loop back to exposure

A submission’s loss history (aeo:LossExperience, AIR-4.4) is what an insured’s claims look like from the next placement. When an insurer holds the claims, the loss history is derived from them:

    DER["derivation: loss run<br/>as of the reporting date"]

The cause chain is the same aeo:LossCause nodes, so nothing is recoded. The figures are a snapshot as of the reporting date, cited by their read set, so a loss run can always be rebuilt and compared with what the insured declared.

14.2 A US D&O securities claim, claims made and notified

A shareholder class action is filed against a listed company and two directors on 2028-05-10, after the company notified circumstances (a restatement) on 2027-11-01, inside the 2027 policy period. The 2028 policy has a prior notice exclusion.

Question How the model answers
which policy responds? the notice of circumstances is deemed to make the later claim a claim made on 2027-11-01 (a relation-back deeming), so the 2027 policy responds and the 2028 policy’s prior notice exclusion applies
who are the parties? claimant: the securities holders (exposure population, AIR-4.5). Liable: the company and two directors. Direction: third party, derived
which sides respond? Side A for non-indemnifiable loss of the directors, Side B for the company’s indemnity of them, Side C for the company’s own securities liability (term-parameters §7.4)
is defence owed? the policy has no duty to defend, and a duty to advance defence costs within 90 days of bills (CCS S1). Each invoice is an advancement occasion
allocation between covered and uncovered parties or matters a computation declared by the policy’s allocation clause, or a finding where it says “fair and proper allocation” (CCS §6.4)
an insured against insured exclusion? does not apply: the claimant is not an insured. A derivative demand by a shareholder for the company would read the derivative direction and its write-back
a consent to settle the insured may not settle without the insurer’s consent, not unreasonably withheld (CCS S30)
a hammer clause if the insured refuses a recommended settlement, the insurer’s liability is capped at that sum plus costs to that date

14.3 A windstorm across a book, with catastrophe reinsurance

A hurricane makes landfall on 2027-09-12 and causes 4,200 losses in a binding authority’s book over three days. The binding authority’s insurer has a catastrophe excess of loss treaty with a 72-hour hours clause and one reinstatement at 100% additional premium.

Step Model
the loss event icm:LossEvent keyed by its PCS catastrophe serial number and GLIDE number
each loss clm:Loss with clm:inLossEvent, coded in the coverholder’s list and lifted exactly where the crosswalk allows (AIR-3.4)
the coverholder’s claims authority each claim within the per-claim authority is settled by the coverholder, above it referred (CCS S46), payments drawn from the loss fund ledger (A12)
the claims bordereau ingested monthly through a MORK mapping (Lloyd’s CRS v5.2)

reinstatement premium an obligation arising on the draw, its amount proRata as to amount (A56)

This is also robustness-testing §4.2’s catastrophe burst: about 130,000 claims in ten days on a market-wide book. The heavy work there is coverage determination per claim, and this example is the shape its benchmark should take.

Two observations from this survey shape the design. First, the open claims standards are message formats, not meanings: ACORD and the London market’s messages say how to send a claim, not what a claim is in law. So the model follows the law and maps the formats, as MORK bridge B4 says. Second, HL7 FHIR’s financial module is the closest open standard to a claims meaning model, and its shape, a claim against a coverage answered by an adjudication per item, is the same shape as a response per instrument with occasions per relation and quanta per loss item. That convergence is a useful check that the response pattern of §7.5 is not peculiar to one market.

19. Questions for the maintainer

Each question names what exists, the options and their consequences, and a leaning. Numbered for the record (CL-Q1 to CL-Q10).

CL-Q1. Does claims import the contract module, or Instrument directly?

ADR-A98 decision 5 says claims imports exposure and contract. The contract module is deferred, and everything §7 needs of contracts is Instrument’s.

Option Design Runtime and roadmap
(a) Instrument directly, the contract module optionally for term parameters claims can be built before Phase 5. An ADR-A98 addendum no change to evaluation. Term parameters, when built, add readings (claims basis, aggregation) without changing claims
(b) wait for the contract module as ADR-A98 says AIR-6.2 waits for Phase 5, which waits for the Open CBAA integration (D2)

Leaning: (a). It follows CCS’s layer flip (applied layers may build on Behaviour and Instrument directly, CCS §7.1) and lets the claims structure be built and checked now.

CL-Q2. When does the indemnity occasion arise?

occasion-refinement.ttl has the indemnity occasion arise at notification. Under English law the property insurer’s liability arises at the loss (§2.1), and the liability insurer’s when the insured’s liability is established.

Option Consequence
(a) at the trigger the governing law gives, chosen by an evaluation profile, with notification and proof as conditions of the duty to pay reads right to a lawyer, and s.13A’s damages run from the right time. Needs two relations (indemnify, pay) where examples now have one
(b) at notification, everywhere simpler, and wrong in law for English first-party cover

Leaning: (a), with the reference profile for English law and a US profile to follow W-Q2’s review. The Behaviour example stays valid as a deployment’s simplification, and its README would say so.

CL-Q3. One claim shared by every instrument, or a claim per insurer?

Option Data Consequence
(a) one claim, a response per instrument version (§7.5) facts once. N responses for N instruments no drift between insurers’ facts. Each insurer’s reserves and positions hang off its response
(b) a claim per insurer, linked facts copied N times matches most insurers’ systems, which only see their own claim. Drift and reconciliation

Quantified for a 10-insurer subscription with 3 layers and one reinsurance: (a) stores the loss, its causes and its documents once and 31 responses. (b) stores them 31 times. Leaning: (a). An insurer that sees only its own share is still served: its view is its response plus the shared facts it is entitled to see, a Persistence profile choice.

CL-Q4. Money now as movements, or wait for the ledger?

Option Consequence
(a) movements now, balances as derived artefacts, the ledger when the evaluation context lands claims can be built and reported now. Erosion is derived from movements and static limits until then
(b) wait for the evaluation context one design, later

Leaning: (a). Movements are facts either way, and the ledger is a later consumer of them.

CL-Q5. Is a settlement an ins:Instrument?

Option Consequence
(a) yes, clm:Settlement ⊑ ins:Instrument reuses wording, terms, assent, breach and amendment. A release is an exclusion that can be checked
(b) a claims-specific record with an amount and a date simpler, and loses the release, the conditions and the consent

Leaning: (a).

CL-Q6. Is the stated coverage position kept apart from the evaluated coverage?

Leaning: yes, always (§7.7). The alternative, recording only the evaluation, loses what the insurer told the insured, which is what a waiver, estoppel, bad faith or conduct question turns on.

CL-Q7. Prefix and module name

Leaning: clm:, clm-voc:, module insurance/claims/, under ADR-A98 decision 6, which leaves the claims prefix to be fixed when the module is built.

CL-Q8. Privilege, retention and legal hold: in AIR, or substrate ADRs first?

Option Consequence
(a) substrate ADRs (S-CL2 to S-CL4) before AIR-6.7 general, clean room, reusable by any domain that holds records
(b) claims-local classes now quicker, and every other domain reinvents them

Leaning: (a), and AIR-6.7 is late in the order, so there is time.

CL-Q9. Reserves: case reserves only?

Leaning: yes. IBNR and portfolio reserving stay with the insurer’s actuarial work, fed by the movements (§9.4). A leftovers row records it.

CL-Q10. Parametric covers: in claims, or in the contract module?

Leaning: the trigger and payout are the contract’s (Phase 5 term parameters and contract amounts A57). The claim, its loss event and the determination are the claims module’s (§7.11), so both are needed and neither waits for the other.
