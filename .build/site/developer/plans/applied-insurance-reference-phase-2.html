The extractor (tools/literate_extract.py)

ADR-A120 serves top-level layers with one vocabulary document. AIR needs nested modules and several vocabulary documents (peril-vocab.ttl, peril-intensity.ttl, peril-collections.ttl).

  1. --layer accepts a path under ontology/, for example applied/insurance/peril. Default targets use its last segment: spec/peril.ttl, vocab/peril-vocab.ttl. Paths without a / behave exactly as today.
  2. The # @output-file "…" directive is read on the first line of a turtle-vocab block as well, and must name a .ttl under the module’s vocab/. A redirected vocabulary document gains the @prefix lines of the default vocabulary document’s blocks, as a redirected spec document does.
  3. A module’s vocabulary documents carry one version, as its spec documents do (ADR-A120 decision 3). Extraction fails if their owl:versionIRI versions differ. A module’s spec and vocabulary versions remain independent of each other.

The documentation rule, for every slice of the trip

Every concept in a scheme the trip publishes (prl-voc:, aeo-voc:, icm-voc:, units) carries:

Property Content
skos:prefLabel, skos:definition (@en) as AIR-2.1 requires
skos:note (@en, at least one) which published standards, scales or sources the concept follows, how it follows them, and where it departs from them. For example: “Follows the IRDR Peril Classification’s tropical cyclone. The basin names follow the WMO regional specialised meteorological centres. Unlike the IRDR entry, storm surge and rainfall flooding are parts of this concept, not separate hazards (PV2).” A concept no standard covers says so and names the usage it follows
rdfs:seeAlso the IRI of a standard’s own entry, where one is stable and public. Never a link to a paywalled or unofficial copy
skos:scopeNote where the concept’s boundary needs saying
skos:exactMatch, skos:closeMatch to an external concept only where AIR-3.4’s crosswalk rules allow, so not in this rule

skos:note was chosen over skos:editorialNote because the alignment is for every reader of the vocabulary, while an editorial note is for its maintainers. Each module README gains a “Sources consulted” section listing every standard the notes cite, with its edition or date.

A new shape, “documentation”, in peril/shapes/constraints.ttl (bumping the shapes .version to 0.2.0, once on the branch): every concept in a prl-voc: scheme has at least one skos:note in @en. Violation. The exposure module’s shapes carry the same rule from AIR-4.1. This slice adds the shape, and the notes on the concepts insurance/common and AIR-2.1 already publish.

Characteristics: inheritance and overrides

A concept takes each characteristic from the nearest concept on its primary-parent chain (itself first) that the tables assign it, starting from the family default. S writes the resulting values onto every concept: the shapes check each concept, and no reasoner fills them in. “None” in an override means the concept, and those below it, carry no value for that optional characteristic. Mechanism, consequence and harm subject inherit the same way, and a list replaces the inherited list, it does not add to it.

Definitions

The peril vocabulary sketch fixes the concepts. Their definitions are guided by published standards, consulted and paraphrased, never copied:

  • the IRDR Peril Classification and Hazard Glossary (2014), whose natural-hazard families (geophysical, hydrological, meteorological, climatological, biological, extraterrestrial) are the N groups
  • the UNDRR and ISC Hazard Information Profiles
  • WMO definitions for tropical cyclone classes and the regional naming authorities
  • the EM-DAT disaster classification
  • the Oasis Open Exposure Data peril codes, for the model-code notations
  • for T, E, H, P, C, F and L, which those sources do not cover, common legal and market usage, stated neutrally

Rules for every skos:definition:

  1. One sentence, genus then difference (“A tropical cyclone whose …”).
  2. What the event or condition is. Never what a policy does with it: no “covered”, “excluded”, “insured”, “loss” in the coverage sense, or market meaning (PV8).
  3. A threshold is named, not numbered (“hurricane strength”). The numbers live in peril-intensity.ttl (AIR-2.6).
  4. A part (link P) is defined as a part of its parent event (“the damaging wind field of a tropical cyclone”).
  5. No text longer than a short phrase from any source. If a source’s wording is the only natural one, reword it.

The module README gains a “Sources consulted” section listing the standards above.

Currencies (decided 2026-10-13, Q14). Exposure, loss and requirement amounts need money units, so common/ declares every active currency of ISO 4217 List One as a qnt:Unit on one money space, icm-voc:Money. Each has its alphabetic code as local name (icm-voc:USD), its numeric code as skos:notation, its English name as skos:prefLabel, and a note citing ISO 4217 and giving its minor unit (2 for USD, 0 for JPY, 3 for KWD). Funds codes and precious metals (XAU, XDR and the like) are left out, and the note on the space says so. No conversion is declared between currencies: a rate depends on a date and a source, so it is a qnt:Conversion under a qnt:ConversionContext (Quantification’s pattern), authored by whoever converts. S drafts the list from the standard. R checks it against the maintenance agency’s published List One at verification and records the edition date in the README’s “Sources consulted”. The month and year are qnt:CalendarUnits (ADR-A94), for horizons and indemnity periods: their length in days depends on the calendar and the date counted from, so any conversion to days is contextual and none is declared.

Vocabulary (vocab/peril-collections.ttl, new, 0.1.0)

A third vocabulary document of the module (AIR-2.0 allows several), with its own README section and @output-file directive. It imports peril-vocab at the branch version. Ontology IRI …/insurance/peril/collections, version IRI …/insurance/peril-collections/0.1.0.

  1. The eight reference collections of sketch §7.3, with the members and notes the table gives, each typed by its kind: prl:AllWind, prl:WindWithSurge, prl:AllFlood and prl:AllEarthquake are model groupings, prl:FireAndExtendedPerils, prl:VandalismAndMaliciousMischief and prl:StrikeRiotCivilCommotion bundles, prl:CBAAStandardExclusions a standard set.
  2. Model groupings for OED’s group peril codes, each with its code as skos:notation typed prl:CatModelCode and members from the cause scheme, for the group codes S is sure of (for example the all-flood and wind-with-surge groups). A model grouping whose members equal one of step 1’s carries the code on that collection, not a second one. R checks the codes against the OED specification, as for AIR-2.3’s notations.
  3. Every collection has a skos:prefLabel, a skos:definition and a note, which cites the market practice or model convention it follows (for example the fire and extended perils bundle follows the London market’s FLEXA and the US extended coverage endorsement, and its note says a market edition overrides it).

The US example edition’s bundles (examples/market-us.ttl)

ex:BasicCausesOfLoss and ex:BroadCausesOfLoss, prl:PerilBundles whose notes cite the ISO commercial property causes of loss forms (CP 10 10 basic, CP 10 20 broad), naming the forms and not quoting them:

Bundle Members
basic T.FIR, N.MET.SCS.LTG, T.EXP, N.MET.LWS, N.MET.SCS.HAI, T.IMP.ACR, T.IMP.VEH, P.PV.RIO, P.PV.CCM, H.MAL.VAN, T.EOW.SPR, N.GEO.MM.SNK, N.GEO.VOL

Smoke, a peril of both forms, is a mechanism (Smoke), so the basic bundle’s note says the form’s smoke peril is read through the mechanism and not as a member. Windstorm is N.MET.LWS here because the forms’ windstorm is any wind, and the note says a market edition may prefer a wider set. The special form is open perils with exclusions (sketch §9.3) and is not a bundle.

Binding-time expansion (tools/peril_binding.py)

Two functions, the code that executes what the vocabulary states:

  • members(graph, collection): the collection’s members, following nested collections. Not their narrower concepts, since a hierarchical condition reaches those itself.
  • open_perils_condition(graph, scheme, condition, exclusions): the triples of a hierarchical condition whose required concepts are the scheme’s top concepts and whose excluded concepts are the members of the given collections and concepts, with elg:wildcardSemantics elg:NoWildcard (ADR-A87).
