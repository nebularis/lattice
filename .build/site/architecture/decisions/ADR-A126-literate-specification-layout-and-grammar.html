ADR-A126: Literate specification layout and grammar

Status: Published, 2026-10-19. Accepted 2026-10-17. Described in: Literate specifications Date: 2026-10-16 Supersedes: none. Adds addenda to ADR-A120 and to ADR-A01’s convention note (2026-10-17) Related: ADR-A01, ADR-A86 and the versioning policy, ADR-A97 (OWL 2 DL), ADR-A120, ADR-A121, ADR-A127, ADR-A-C2 (examples first), conventions document

Context

A layer’s README.md is its authoritative specification, and its Turtle is extracted from fenced blocks in document order. Three problems have grown with the layers.

  • Facts are written more than once, and the copies drift. A term’s Definition and Utility are written in prose and again as rdfs:comment and fnd:utility strings in its block, and for fnd:Version the two already read differently. Vocabulary states voc:ConceptScheme’s superclass skos:ConceptScheme in an alignment block, in a Utility paragraph and in an axiom index row, and not in the class’s own block.
  • Practice differs by layer. READMEs order and name their sections differently. Instrument checks 91 counts in SHACL that no OWL restriction states, and Quantification asserts 50 in OWL that no shape checks.
  • Blocks make READMEs hard to read. Many turtle-spec blocks exist only for the extractor, and a reader who wants the Turtle can open the generated file.

About 2,700 axiom statements make up the literate layers’ spec and vocab documents (measured 2026-10-15). Fewer than 30 of them need more than a label, a short list of class expressions or a restriction table to state.

Decision

  1. One layout for every module README.

    § Section Required Normative Extracted to
    1 Purpose and Scope yes yes the ontology’s dct:description
    2 Namespace and Prefixes yes yes prefixes, the ontology header, creators and contributors
    3 Extraction Contract yes yes the module’s repository catalogue entry (ADR-A121)
    4 Overview yes no nothing. Holds a generated class diagram
    5 Detailed Design yes no nothing
    6 Description Logic Encoding yes yes spec and vocab documents
    7 Shapes yes yes shape files
    8 Examples yes no nothing. Examples stay hand-written files
    9 Canonicalisation no yes nothing
    10 Formalisation no yes isabelle-spec blocks and what is generated from them
    11 Dependencies yes no nothing
    12 Release Notes yes yes nothing. Checked
    13 Open Issues yes no nothing

    Section 6 has the subsections 6.1 Classes, 6.2 Object Properties, 6.3 Datatype Properties, 6.4 Individuals, 6.5 Disjointness and Keys, 6.6 Other Axioms and 6.7 Axiom Index. Numbers are fixed. An absent optional section keeps its heading and says “Not applicable.”, and an empty subsection says “None.” A layer adds no top-level section of its own. Other material goes into a subsection of Detailed Design or of Open Issues. The extractor finds a section by its title and warns when its number differs from the layout.

  2. Normative sections win. Where a non-normative section disagrees with a normative one, the normative section is the specification. Overview and Detailed Design never introduce an axiom.
  3. Section 6 is written in a grammar, and is the only source of a term’s axioms. An entry is a level-4 heading holding one CURIE, followed by labelled paragraphs and tables, for example **Definition.** for rdfs:comment, **Utility.** for fnd:utility, **Label.** for rdfs:label, **Subclass of.**, **Equivalent to.**, **Domain.**, **Range.**, **Characteristics.**, **Inverse of.**, **Subproperty of.**, **Key.** and **Restrictions.**. A further label adds a further annotation, for example **Scope note.** for skos:scopeNote. An unknown label is an error. rdfs:label comes only from a **Label.** line.
  4. Class expressions are OWL Manchester syntax with CURIEs, wherever one appears: superclasses, equivalent classes, domains, ranges and the values of a restriction. Literals use Turtle’s forms.
  5. A restriction row states where it is asserted: in OWL and SHACL (the default), in OWL only, or in SHACL only. Some OWL restrictions are hints to the reasoner for design-time tooling, so an OWL restriction is also checked in SHACL unless the row says otherwise. SHACL is generated only for the flat forms (exactly, min, max, their qualified forms, only, some and value), and a nested expression is refused unless its row says OWL only. A qualified restriction maps to SHACL’s qualified form, never to sh:class with counts, since the two say different things. Generated SHACL goes into one shape per class in the module’s structural shapes file. Hand-written shapes stay in blocks in section 7.
  6. What the grammar cannot say stays a block. Section 6.6 holds turtle-spec blocks for the rest, for example property chains.
  7. Generated regions. The Axiom Index and the Overview’s class diagram are generated from section 6 into marked regions of the README, written in place and kept current by the extractor’s check. A hand edit inside a region is reported by the check and lost at the next extraction.
  8. HTML comment markers do four things. They exclude text from an extracted region, hide a fenced block that is still extracted, send a region of entries to another file, and mark a generated region. A region opens with <!-- start: {...} --> and closes with <!-- end -->, which closes the innermost open region. A hidden block is one comment wrapped round a fence. The keys are exclude, output-file and generated, and any other key is an error. A marker inside a fenced block is text. A hidden block may not contain -->, and mermaid is never hidden.
  9. One name, output-file, in two forms. It is the # @output-file first line of a fenced block, as ADR-A120 defines it, or the JSON key of a marker. A block that carries both is refused. Both forms apply to every extracted block kind.
  10. Extraction rules.
    • Extracted markdown becomes plain text. Links become their text and an absolute URL at the release tag that the section 2 version names.
    • Generated Turtle is sorted by subject and then by a fixed predicate order, so reorganising a README changes no generated file. A document’s fenced blocks follow its generated statements, ordered by their own text, and declare no prefix, since section 2’s prefix table is the one source of prefixes (clarified 2026-10-18).
    • Dublin Core terms are declared in each document as annotation properties, with the dct: prefix, and never imported, so every document stays in OWL 2 DL.
    • Creators and contributors come from a table in section 2 that the maintainers keep. Each is identified by an IRI, an ORCID iD where the person has one and their GitHub profile otherwise. No legal name appears in a README or a generated artefact without that person’s express permission.
  11. Release notes are checked, not extracted. Every version named in section 2, and every released version, has an entry. An entry says what changed and cites only published ADRs (ADR-A127).
  12. Versioning is unchanged. Generated Turtle stays tracked and versioned under ADR-A86. A layer’s first regeneration under this layout is one PATCH release, checked to be the same graph as before except for reconciliations the maintainer approves.
  13. One conventions document. docs/architecture/literate-specifications.md describes the layout, the grammar, the markers and the extraction rules. Each README’s section 3 links to it and states only what is specific to its module. The tool documentation, the ontology architecture document, the glossary and the ontology authoring skill link to it rather than restating it.

Consequences

  • ADR-A120 has an addendum for decision 9, and ADR-A01 one for its convention note, which placed the ontology header in a README’s first turtle-spec block (both 2026-10-17).
  • Each layer is migrated in its own change, with its own PATCH release. Until a layer is migrated, its README keeps today’s conventions and literate_extract.py --check keeps testing it as now.
  • The 76 restrictions asserted in OWL with no shape today migrate as OWL only, so migration adds no shape and no new violation.
  • A layer with no release notes today gets them rebuilt from its history when it migrates. Existing notes that cite delivery slices are rewritten to say what changed.
  • Foundation, Vocabulary and Party are not yet the source of their documents. Each is reconciled before or during its migration, with every difference listed for the maintainer.
  • The extractor stays standard-library only. The grammar is line-based so that a small parser suffices, including a recursive-descent parser for the Manchester subset in use.
  • Persistence, MORK, the applied modules and Governance are not literate today. They take the layout when they become literate.
