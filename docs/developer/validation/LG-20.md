## Handoff

Built on machine S, 2026-11-16, Isabelle2025-2 through its bundled Cygwin, committed locally and not pushed. One definition of the kernel changed, `post`, as LG-Q29 (b) leans: a credit whose account does not accept its kind is refused. The README's block, the spike's `Target.thy` and the model changed together, `Kernel.thy` was extracted again, the spike's cases were regenerated and both spike sessions build, and every claim's digest moved with the kernel.

**What was built.** All fifty-one of the plan's laws, each restating the plan's sentence and proved. The first thirty-six were committed first. LB1n, LU1n, LE4 and LE5 followed, with LU3n as a counterexample to LU3 as planned. Then `post` changed, and LU3, LW4, LH1, LH2, LM2, LJ2, LJ3, LP2, LP4 and LPn were built, with LU3n restated, and LP1 last, on the maintainer's condition.

The first part built thirty-six, and what follows describes them unless it says otherwise. Every group has some built, and the predicates and ranks are whole. Supporting lemmas carry the structure the laws share: `Ranks.first_misfit_exists` and `Ranks.by_rank_sum_split`, which split by rank at the first rank whose requests do not fit, `Ranks.by_rank_exact_where_no_tie`, which LM3 rests on, `Units.units_count`, `Units.placed_larger_remainder_le`, `Credits.run_sum` and `Readings.counted_split`. `Credits.trace` and `Units.three_none_eleven`, `Units.none_first`, `Merge.facility`, `Credits.one_account`, `Credits.three_requests` and `Readings.late_debit` are definitions in the theorem theories, for LB2 and the instances.

**Statements and their hypotheses.** Each states the plan's law with the hypotheses it needs, and none is weaker than the plan's sentence.

- LK asks the rule for a tie to commute with the renaming, since by rank hands it the tied requests as they are named.
- LT1 asks the rule for a tie to be lawful and the amount from zero to what was asked, since with a rule that leaves a residual one rank of one request is granted what is left where the rule might grant less.
- LT2 asks the rule for a tie to ration, or be lawful, on every set of the keys, since by rank hands it whichever rank does not fit.
- LW3 asks a key set that is not empty, since with no key the residual is zero and so not less than their number.
- LW5 states "the leftover covers every key at the least remainder it would reach" as the number of keys largest remainder places with no order equal to the units short.
- LJ1 asks a proposal list that is not empty, since with none every account is in the one set's room.
- LJ5 counts the purposes among the requests as the set of purposes some request names.
- LE1 asks every posting recorded by the recording to be effective by the day, which is what makes the recorded balance reading A at that recording.
- LP3 asks every pot to be under the fund.
- LE4 states "every posting is effective when recorded" as the effective days of the postings being 1 to their number, and asks a tree with every draw's account in it, since reading B settles a draw again through `available`.
- LE5 asks a tree with every posted draw's account in it and its amount non-negative, and the stated amounts from zero to the ceiling. Its proof carries the range through `replay` (`Readings.replay_in_range`), and LE4's carries the run's balances (`Readings.replay_run`).
- LU3 asks a tree with every request's account in it and every credit requested at a leaf, an account of the tree no other lies below. Its proof carries the invariant that each account's room is at least the rooms of the leaves below it together (`Credits.lu3_run`). `credit` alone needs the leaf to accept the kind, which `post` now secures.
- LW4 states the renaming as LK does, the requests read through the inverse of the bijection.
- LH1 asks a finite key set with weights of positive sum, and preferences that are strict total orders on the shares rounded down and on those rounded up. LH2 asks the same of the weights and a key in the set, and states "within one unit" as the grant times the weights' sum less the share's numerator being less than the sum in absolute value.
- LM2 asks a tree with the account in it, one rank, a policy that neither refuses nor asks for ranks, the tie rule largest remainder in an order injective on the proposers, requests non-negative and the path's balances non-negative. It states "within one unit" as LH2 does, and only where something is asked.
- LJ2 asks each set the requests' accounts are limited by to be in the list the merge is given and within the proposers, each request's reach finite with non-negative balances, the requests non-negative and the tie rule, where there is one, rationing on every set.
- LJ3 asks the sets given to be pairwise nested or apart, as refusing crossing sets secures where they are the limited sets, and within the proposers, with LJ2's conditions on the tie rule and the requests. A merge refuses for one reason under one policy (`Pots.merge_of_reason`), so the order of two disjoint sets cannot change the reason.
- LP2 and LP4 ask every pot in the tree, the requests non-negative, nothing held at the start for LP2, and the tie rule, where there is one, rationing on every set. LP2 states "no pot is paid more than its own amount" as each pot's grants being at most its account's balance before the pots. LP4 is stated for the first pot, which every pot is of the pots after it: where what was asked exceeds what is available and what is available is the fund's, no later pot is paid.
- LPn's two instances use a rule that grants each request whole or not at all and leaves the rest.
- LP1 states "no pot's own amount binds" as the maintainer put it on 2026-11-16: every account on a pot's path below the fund holds at least what the fund makes available before the pots run. It asks a tie rule that rations on every set, the pots' groups apart, the rank `by_rank` reads being each group's place in the list, and the requests each group's. The induction (`Pots.pots_by_groups`) is stated for any amount held and any balances, the condition read against what the fund makes available after the held amount is withheld, so a step keeps it: an account below the fund falls only by what earlier pots were paid through it, while the fund's figure falls by all that was paid and held. A pot granted in full leaves the next what `by_rank` leaves it, and a pot cut short leaves nothing (`Pots.by_rank_nothing_left`). The maintainer's search on machine R found pots sharing an account below the fund in about 57,500 of 100,000 cases a row, so the condition does the work and the shape of the tree does not. A pot holding less than the fund's figure is left out, where earlier pots take enough that it still does not bind, and LP2 to LP4 still apply there.

**Findings.** LU3 was false as the plan stated it, raised as LG-Q29. A credit's applied amount is bounded by the room at the account it is posted to, whether or not that account accepts the credit's kind. A leaf of 10 that does not accept a kind, under a parent of 10 that does, drawn 5 and credited 5 twice, was cut by 5 at the parent, with every ceiling equal to its stated amount and every credit on a leaf. The model's search could not have drawn it, since `search_history` skipped a credit whose account does not accept its kind. The maintainer leans (b), and `post` now refuses such a credit, so LU3 as the plan states it holds of what a run posts and is proved. LU3n keeps the case for `credit` alone. The model's search now draws refused credits and checks LU3 on every history, 2,631 credits at a scale of 20.

LJn evaluates by `code_simp` with accounts of type `bool`, since `room` and `crossing` range over every account of the type, as the spike's cases do.
