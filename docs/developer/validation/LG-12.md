## Invariant

An account accepts a draw only in its own value space and unit, and only where its space can represent the amount, and a draw it does not accept is refused, never converted and never rounded, with the reason Quantification gives for the same comparison.

## What a draw is in the vocabulary

A draw is a **debit posting** (`ldg:Posting` with `ldg:direction ldg:Debit`), and a **request on a merge** (`ldg:Request`, held by a merge record with `ldg:request`), with what it asked (`ldg:requestedValue`) and what it was granted (`ldg:grantedValue`). A request states no direction, so the request of a credit refused alone (`refusal-credit-kind`) is checked too, and it conforms. A credit posting is not a draw and is not checked.

ADR-A131 decision 4 states a posting's amount as "a decimal literal in the account's unit of account, never a quantity node of its own", and LG-11 removed the only terms that stated an amount as drawn. So no term lets a draw state another value space or unit for its own amount, and that half of the rule has two parts:

- **What a draw read.** The only thing a draw can bring in another space or unit is a quantity named in its read set, `prov:wasDerivedFrom`. A draw that read a `qnt:Quantity` outside its account's space or unit took an amount the ledger would have to convert, or a proportion it would have to resolve. A record made before the ledger, a conversion or a proportion's resolution, is not a quantity, so a draw may cite one, as the revolving facility's postings cite their conversion records.
- **The account's unit.** The account's value space is the one its definition names with `ldg:unitOfAccount`. Its unit is the one its stated amount is in, `qnt:inUnit`, where the space has units, since a space such as the facility's money holds sterling and euros alike. Two quantities with no unit are in one unit.

A draw's amount is **representable** where it is a decimal (its literal is `xsd:decimal` or a datatype derived from it), its value is in the value space of the space's `qnt:valueDatatype`, `xsd:decimal` where none is stated, and, on a space whose `qnt:densityKind` is `qnt:Discrete` and that states a `qnt:granularityFloor`, it is a whole multiple of the floor. A space whose datatype is not `xsd:decimal` or derived from it, `xsd:double` for one, represents no ledger amount.

### Shapes added, and the reason each gives

| Shape | Refuses | Reason | Quantification's term |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ldg:DrawInItsAccountsSpaceShape` | a debit posting or a request whose `prov:wasDerivedFrom` names a `qnt:Quantity` not on its account's unit of account | `qnt:OutsideDeclaredSpace` | yes. Quantification's minimum corpus (`examples/examples.md`, section 24) gives it for "candidate and bound on distinct spaces" |
| `ldg:DrawInItsAccountsUnitShape` | the same, where the quantity is on the account's space and in another unit than its stated amount, or one states a unit and the other none | "no permitted conversion" | no. Quantification's section 5.1.7 ends the case in "Undetermined, no permitted conversion", and its section 9.6 in Undetermined with no term. The shape's message uses that wording, and the ledger permits no conversion at all |
| `ldg:DrawRepresentableShape` | a debit posting or a request whose amount is not a decimal, is outside the value space of its space's datatype, or on a discrete space with a floor is not a whole multiple of it | `qnt:OutsideDeclaredSpace` | yes, as the nearest. Quantification has no term for an amount that is not representable, and does not yet check a literal against its space's datatype (TD-125). 3.3 licences is not among the values a space of whole licences declares, so it is outside the declared space. This reading is a question below |

Each shape targets `ldg:Posting` and `ldg:Request` as nodes and reads every instance in one query, as the other runtime laws do. Each message names its reason. SHACL forbids `VALUES` in a SPARQL constraint, so the datatypes derived from `xsd:decimal` and their bounds are written out in the filter: `xsd:integer`, the four sign-bounded integers, `xsd:long`, `xsd:int`, `xsd:short`, `xsd:byte` and the four unsigned ones.

### Terms added and changed

No term was added or removed. The three shapes are new, and the utilities of `ldg:unitOfAccount`, `ldg:Posting` and `ldg:amountValue` changed.

## Deliberate non-coverage

- **A credit, a cut, a residual and a balance** are not checked for representability, and a credit's read set is not checked for its space and unit. The plan's row and LG-Q20 name draws. Section 13 records it.
- **A discrete space with no granularity floor.** Every discrete space in the existing examples states none, so only the datatype limits an amount on it. Whether a discrete unit of account must state a floor is open (section 13), and a question below.
- **The account's own quantities.** No shape checks that a definition's stated amount and ceiling are on its unit of account, or in one unit. They fix what the account's unit is, and are configuration, outside this slice.
- **A draw's purpose posting.** A draw that names a purpose writes a posting at each account counting it, and no shape checks that those accounts share the drawn account's unit of account. The configuration does not say which accounts a purpose's draws come from.
- **The refusal is not recorded.** A refused draw is refused by the runtime laws with Quantification's reason in the message. No record of the refusal is written, and no reason is added to `ldg:RefusalReasons`, since a draw that is not accepted should be refused before it reaches a merge.
- **The status records, the board, the plan and the debt register** are not edited. The coordinator does.

## Questions for the maintainer

1. **Is a draw's read set the right place to find a draw in another space or unit?** The vocabulary gives a draw's amount no space or unit of its own, so the shapes read the quantities it names in `prov:wasDerivedFrom`. The other reading is that the first half of the rule holds by construction, and only representability needs a shape.
2. **Is `qnt:OutsideDeclaredSpace` the reason for an amount that is not representable?** Quantification has no term for it. If it should have one, it is Quantification's to add, and the shape's message would follow.
3. **Must a discrete unit of account state a granularity floor?** None of the existing examples does, so on them density refuses nothing. A configuration constraint would make the floor required, and the examples would gain one.
4. **Should a credit be held to the same rule?** A credit is also an amount in the account's unit, and a repayment can arrive in euros.
