## Invariant

Each owner has at most one `ldg:Ledger`, which names its `ldg:LedgerProfile` by an edge that bears meaning and states its revision. A closing recurrence is the profile's and no account definition states one. A pass writes at least one record, every record on an account of one owner, and that owner has a ledger. The passes of one ledger record distinct revisions, a later pass a greater one, each one more than another's above the least, and the ledger's revision is the greatest. A checkpoint's debits and accepted credits offered are the sums over the passes recorded by its close, and a checkpoint is written only for an account that moved: its balance or a total differs from its account's previous checkpoint, or from the stated amount with nothing drawn or offered where it has none.

### Choices this slice made

- **The ledger is in the runtime document and its profile in the configuration.** The sketch draws both in its configuration diagram. A ledger holds the revision that every write changes and is minted from its owner, as an account instance is, and a design-time check reads only the configuration document, so the ledger sits with the records. The profile is authored by the deployment and versioned, as a merge policy is. A gap is recorded.
- **`ldg:ledgerProfile` is exactly one.** LG-Q24 makes the profile one of the rules a deployment sets "for every ledger it runs", and LG-Q37 has the ledger name it "by a pinned edge", marked here as `fnd:meaningBearing`, since the profile says whether a ceiling above a stated amount is admitted.
- **`ldg:passRevision`, and no edge from a pass to its ledger.** One triple a pass is the decided cost (LG-Q37), and the second addendum of 2026-11-19 says "no triple is added". So a pass's ledger is read from its records, the ledger of the owner of the accounts they are on, and the laws read it so. A pass that writes no record has no ledger, and the addendum says such an evaluation writes no pass, so `ldg:PassWritesOneLedgerShape` refuses one.
- **The naming rule is stated in prose, with no template.** The README says to derive a pass's IRI from its ledger's and a key the caller gives, and a ledger's from its owner's, as it says a checkpoint's IRI is derived from its account and close position. The examples use `<ledger>/pass/<key>`, and a test holds every example to it. No shape checks an IRI. A gap is recorded.
- **Revisions are consecutive, starting anywhere.** The addendum says a request moves the revision "once, by one". So within a ledger the passes, in the order of their positions, record revisions one apart, and the ledger is at the greatest. Where a ledger's revision starts is not decided, so the least is not checked, and the examples start at 1.
- **The totals are cumulative.** STD-331 says "the debits and the accepted credits offered that were recorded by its close", so each total counts every posting in the account's subtree by passes at or before the close, as the balance does. A reading from a close adds what was recorded after it, which LE3 proves of the flows.
- **"Moved" is read from the checkpoints.** Every posting that moves an account under the addendum's definition raises its debits or its accepted credits offered, and the totals and the balance are checked against the postings by the other two laws. So the law compares a checkpoint with its account's previous one, or with the stated amount and nothing drawn or offered, and reads no posting.
- **A closing recurrence on a definition is refused.** With the restriction gone and the domain moved, nothing would refuse one, so `ldg:ClosingRecurrenceOnProfileShape` does, which also says how to move a document.
- **The refusal of a ceiling above a stated amount is not built.** LG-Q24 and the first addendum of 2026-11-16 have a design-time shape refuse it unless the profile admits it. An account definition names no ledger, so a check of the configuration cannot reach the profile. The term and its default are declared, and section 13 records that no shape refuses such a ceiling. No example states one. A gap and a question are recorded.
- **Section 13 is repaired.** At `HEAD` its first issue had lost its opening, `- **P`, and the issue on a purpose under a shared ancestor, which section 5.1 still points to, had gone, and an issue proposed the refusal reasons that LG-26 declared. All three are put right here, beside the two new issues.

## Deliberate non-coverage

- **A ceiling above a stated amount is not refused** where the profile does not admit it. Section 13 records it, and the question below asks where the check belongs.
- **A close is not checked to write every account that moved.** The records name a close only by its checkpoints, so the law refuses a checkpoint where the account did not move and does not refuse a close that leaves one out. `ledger_corpus` checkpoints its root and sections and not its leaves, which moved. Section 13 records it.
- **No IRI is checked.** No shape asks that a pass's IRI be derived from its ledger and a key, or a ledger's from its owner. LG27-03 holds the examples to the form they use.
- **Where a ledger's revision starts** is not decided, so the least revision of its passes is not checked.
- **A close is not checked against the profile's closing recurrence.** A close position is a pass position, and a recurrence's bins are not related to it in the records.
- **What PDD-22 and PDD-23 leave open** is not built: no revision on a posting, no receipt for each posting, and no term for a read over a set of ledgers. The writer's rule of the second addendum of 2026-11-19 is a rule for a runtime and adds no term.
- **The status records, the board and the plan** are not edited. The coordinator does.

## Questions for the maintainer

1. **Where the refusal of a ceiling above a stated amount belongs**, since a definition reaches no profile at design time: a runtime law over an account, its owner's ledger and the profile, or a link from the configuration to the profile.
2. **`ldg:passRevision`** as the name of the revision a pass records, and **`ldg:Ledger` in the runtime document**.
3. **Whether a close must be checked to checkpoint every account that moved**, which needs the closes to be named in the records.
4. **Whether a ledger's first pass records revision 1**, so the least revision can be checked.
