# Validation Pack: MSA-I1, the census of every shapes graph

**Unit:** [`multi-store-architecture`](../status/multi-store-architecture.md)
**Plan:** [multi-store-architecture](../plans/multi-store-architecture.md), MSA-I1 and its proposed threshold
**Machine:** a Windows laptop, Python from the worktree's `.venv` through mise, rdflib. **Placement:** T0, public. A measurement of the ontology tree, nothing domain-specific.
**Status:** authored and run, not accepted. The maintainer's review is the sign-off, and the threshold is still a proposal.

## Scope

- `spikes/multi-store-shape-census/census.py`, which reads every RDF file under `ontology/`, classifies each node shape against seven criteria and measures the share of the typed subjects of the sample graphs that each class of shape reaches, per module.
- `spikes/multi-store-shape-census/README.md`, the method and its limits, and `results/summary.md`, the tables. The full record, `results/census.json`, is over a megabyte, is regenerated by the script and is not committed.
- The root and the module list come from the repository catalogue reader (STD-273).
- Out of scope. Any change to a shapes graph, and any validation of data.

## Result in one paragraph

The falsifier fires as the sketch states it. No node shape of the 505 in the estate is closed and bounded, so closed and bounded shapes reach 0% of the typed subjects of the sample graphs in every module, against the proposed 80%. Only one shape declares `sh:closed true`, and its property shapes carry no `sh:maxCount`. The near-miss is large and points at a convention rather than at the shapes. 134 shapes fail only on not being closed, and shapes that are bounded but open reach 43.4% of all typed subjects, 73.6% in persistence and 100% in v-rig. The criterion that would remain once closure became a convention is `sh:sparql`, on 250 shapes, which the census records as a failure although a SPARQL constraint may lower to a `CHECK`, a trigger or a run over a view (sketch §4.5). So the census does not show that the mapping is not worth building. It shows that the estate does not state, today, the closure a mapping needs, and that half of the shapes carry constraints whose lowering is MSA-I10's question.

## Adversarial probes

1. The classification is only as strict as its weakest criterion. 34 of the 134 bounded-but-open shapes have no property shapes and pass the bounded test vacuously, so the summary gives the bounded-or-better share a second time without them, 36.9% overall against 43.4%.
2. Following `rdfs:subClassOf` moves the "any shape" share in mork from 15.4% to 72.8% and little elsewhere. Both variants are reported, so the walk cannot hide a gap.
3. Shapes nested in `sh:or`, `sh:node` and `sh:property` are counted once with their parent and not as shapes of their own, which keeps 505 from being inflated by anonymous shapes.

## Decisions taken

- **Closed means `sh:closed true`.** The sketch's §4.4 needs a shape that says what a row holds and nothing else, and an open shape says only what it requires. A looser reading would measure what the census was asked to measure less well.
- **Shapes in sample graphs are not used.** Seven example files declare shapes. They are fixtures written to show a feature and are listed, not counted.
- **Test fixtures are excluded.** They hold deliberately invalid shapes.
- **The script finds the catalogue reader by walking up for `tools/lattice_repo_catalogue.py`**, because the reader is a stand-alone file and not a package.

## Open matters

- The threshold is a proposal awaiting the maintainer. At any threshold above 0 the strict reading fails.
- Whether closure should become a convention of the shapes estate, which would turn 134 shapes row-mappable at once, is a question for the maintainer and for MSA-I10, not a change this slice makes.
- `Mork.owl` does not parse with rdflib ("Repeat node-elements inside property elements"), so mork's classes and subclass links from it are missing. Mork's share is a lower bound.
- `ontology/examples/saas-subscription.ttl` is empty.
- The sample graphs are fixtures, and the shares say how much of the fixtures the shapes reach, not how much of any production data.
