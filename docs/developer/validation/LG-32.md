# Validation Pack: LG-32, what a supersession costs a balance read

**Unit:** `ledger` ([plan](../plans/ledger.md), LG-32, [status](../status/ledger.md)).
**Placement:** T0, public substrate.
**Brief:** the cost of the lookup that a record of a later version adds to every read of a balance, which [LG-Q52](../sketches/ledger-definition-versions-in-force.md) leans on and has argued and not measured.
**Decisions:** none. The benchmark is run under [ADR-A124](../../architecture/decisions/ADR-A124-benchmark-root.md), and it declares its own supersession terms under a prefix of its own, since the layer declares none yet.

**Status:** built and run 2026-11-23, not verified by the maintainer.

## Invariant

Every way of writing a supersession gives the model's balance at every read. A balance read as of a revision under per-account, per-account-with-balance, per-version and per-pass records equals the balance a plain model computes from the event stream alone, and a change of terms writes the triples the variant's arithmetic gives.

## What the benchmark is

[`benchmark/ledger-supersession-read`](../../../benchmark/ledger-supersession-read/README.md) holds one owner and its ledger, an account tree of 15 definitions and 1,014 accounts, N events, and K changes of terms that each raise the aggregate and the 13 definitions below it. The same history is held four ways, and V (the stated amount in force), R1 (a leaf's balance now), R2 (a leaf's balance as of earlier revisions), R3 (an ancestor's balance now) and B (every stated amount now, in one query) are read on Oxigraph, Jena and rdflib at 2,000 and 20,000 events with K = 0, 1 and 10.

## Deliberate non-coverage

- **Periods.** Every account is in one period.
- **A lowered amount.** Every change raises, so the greatest revision and the greatest amount coincide, and no read relies on that.
- **A tree with more than one keyed definition.** One sublimit is keyed, by site.
- **An account minted after a change.** Every account is opened under the first version.
- **The multi-store PostgreSQL arm.** Only triple stores ran.
- **Concurrency.** One writer and one reader.
- **A K above 10.** The reads grow with K where a record is found by an object, and the figures say how for K = 1 and 10 only.
- **Which variant to choose.** This pack makes no recommendation. That is the maintainer's decision.

## What was run, in full

Run of 2026-11-23 on a laptop with 8 GB of memory, Python 3.14.7, pyoxigraph 0.5.13, Jena ARQ 5.1.0 and rdflib 7.6.0, 1,000 sites, 30 repetitions of each read. All three engines ran.

- `mise run check:ledger-supersession-read`: the tests gave `19 passed` (the nine functions, parametrised), the benchmark exited 0 after 2,164 s, and all 72 histories (4 variants, 2 sizes, 3 values of K, 3 engines) matched the model and the arithmetic.
- `mise exec -- python -m pytest benchmark/ledger-supersession-read -q -p no:cacheprovider`, on its own: `19 passed`.

The figures for Jena and rdflib, at 2,000 events, and every 95th percentile are in the report and the README.

- **R3 on Oxigraph rises by 30 to 40% between K = 0 and K = 1 under every variant.** It does not depend on the records. A probe, not part of the benchmark, read 106.3 ms on a store loaded in bulk, 122.6 ms after one unrelated triple was added by `extend`, and 108.9 ms for a K = 1 history loaded wholly in bulk. So the timed write of the last change accounts for part of it, and we did not find the rest.
- **The form of B changed its time by orders of magnitude on two engines.** Three forms returned the model's value, and the one kept took 0.03 s on Oxigraph, 0.13 s on Jena and 1.6 s on rdflib under per-version at K = 10 and 2,000 events. The others took up to 27 s on rdflib and 40.7 s on Oxigraph.
- **V is lower at K = 1 than at K = 0 under every variant on Oxigraph and Jena.** At K = 0 each V finds no record and reads the account's own version in a second query, and at K = 1 an account that has a record needs one query. We did not test this reading.

## Index report

- **Asked:** `find seek a posting by a bound subject, latest posting of an account --kind standard --activity query-writing`, which returned STD-338, STD-330 and RDF-1 and RDF-8, and `read TSD-36`. The answer was weak (best score 0.67), and none of the entries said which form of a latest-record lookup or a greatest-per-group lookup runs well on each engine.
- **Found by reading:** a greatest-per-group join back to the record, which `MINUS` for the groups with none made fast on all three engines, where an optional part (27 s on rdflib) and `FILTER NOT EXISTS` (40.7 s on Oxigraph) were not. No register entry holds this, and it is a candidate finding for the triple-store discovery record.
